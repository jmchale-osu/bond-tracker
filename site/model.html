What should this bond have priced at?

A regression of new issue spread on the bond's rating, maturity, size, structure, its issuer's past deals and the index that day. Every bond is scored out of sample: the model only sees deals priced in earlier weeks, then gives its fair spread before looking at the answer.

Does it beat a simple rule?

The baseline is what you could do with no model: the index spread for the bond's rating that day, plus the median gap to the index for bonds of a similar maturity. Both are scored the same way, week by week.

Came wide of the model

Of the most recent 150 bonds scored, the ones that priced furthest over their fair spread, one per issuer: cheap for the buyer, expensive for the issuer.

Came inside the model

The same bonds, the ones that priced furthest through fair value.

What moves a new issue spread

The model fit on every bond. Each number is the extra spread, in bps, holding the other terms constant. The range is the 5th to 95th percentile when the model is refit on resampled issuers, the same test the thesis scorecard uses. The five structure terms are the thesis, asked a second way.
