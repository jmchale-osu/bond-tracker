How the tracker works

Where the numbers come from, how the thesis is tested, and what the data cannot tell you.

How each claim is tested

  • Spread vs index. Each bond's spread to Treasuries minus the ICE BofA option-adjusted index spread for its rating on the day it priced. Positive means it came wider, which is cheaper for the buyer.
  • Peers. A bond with the feature is compared first with the same issuer's bonds sold the same day in the same maturity bucket, on raw spread, which holds the company constant. Failing that, with bonds that lack the feature in the same rating bucket (AA, A, BBB, BB) and maturity bucket, on spread vs index.
  • Holding ranking constant. The change of control and coupon step claims compare senior unsecured bonds only, so they do not pick up subordination or collateral.
  • Range. The 5th to 95th percentile of the median across 1,000 resamples. Issuers are resampled whole, because twelve bonds from one deal are one piece of evidence, not twelve.
  • Verdict. Holding if the whole range sits on the expected side of zero, Not holding if it sits on the other side, Mixed if it crosses zero. Too early below 5 bonds or 3 issuers.
  • Timing. The Market page tests one more idea the same way: whether senior unsecured bonds priced on or the business day before a Fed decision, CPI report or jobs report come wider than peers priced on other days. It is kept off the scorecard because timing is not a prospectus term.
  • Trend. The estimate recomputed with only the deals priced by the end of each week, so you can see whether a reading is settling or still moving.

Limits

  • Registered deals only. Most high yield is sold under Rule 144A and never reaches EDGAR, so HY samples are small.
  • Index spreads are matched by rating category, not exact notch, and do not adjust for sector or call features.
  • Covenants are not assigned at random: issuers offer protection when buyers ask for it, so a feature's premium partly reflects the kind of company that offers it.
  • Filings do not disclose order books, price talk or the new issue concession.
  • The timing test uses scheduled dates, not surprises: a CPI report that matches forecasts counts the same as one that shocks the market. Issuers also choose when to come, so the bonds priced on data days are not a random sample.
  • 2637 of 2651 tranches have a stated ranking. 56 failed the pricing math check and are flagged, not discarded.

How it is built

Every source is free and every step is a rule you can read, so the weekly update runs with no API key and gives the same answer each time it runs.

Findbackfill.py searches EDGAR full text for free writing prospectuses that carry a bond's pricing terms ("Spread to Benchmark Treasury" and its variants), going back to November 2024.
Readparse_fwp.py reads each term sheet's tables: size, coupon, maturity, CUSIP, spread, yield, price, benchmark, make-whole and par call, ratings. Checked against an earlier hand-verified extraction of 215 bonds, it finds 213 and matches coupon and maturity on all of them, yield and price on 99%.
Prospectus onlyAmazon, Oracle and Microsoft file no term sheet. prospectus_deals.py takes each tranche's price from the final prospectus's filing fee table and computes yield and spread over the Treasury curve. These are marked as computed and kept out of the index comparison.
Structurestructure.py and rules.py read ranking, guarantees, change of control and coupon steps from the term sheet and the prospectus supplement, checked against the notes' titles.
Proceedsproceeds.py classifies use of proceeds by the deal it names: a named acquisition, named debt to repay, or general corporate purposes.
CheckEvery tranche must satisfy benchmark yield + spread = yield. dedupe.py merges a bond filed twice, matched on CUSIP or on issuer, coupon and maturity.
Enrichfred.py and enrich.py pull index spreads and the Treasury curve from FRED. Only US dollar fixed rate bonds are compared with the index: a floater's spread is to SOFR and a euro bond's is to Bunds.
Macromacro.py pulls inflation, jobs and the fed funds target from FRED, Fed decision days from federalreserve.gov and CPI and jobs report dates from ALFRED.
Testthesis.py runs the matched comparisons and resampling behind the scorecard.
Modelmodel.py fits the fair value regression each week on earlier deals only and scores the new ones.
Publishreport.py builds this site.
