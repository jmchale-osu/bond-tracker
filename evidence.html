Subordination is paid for

Too early   Expected: wider

In a bankruptcy, creditors are paid in order of rank. Senior unsecured holders are paid before subordinated holders, and junior subordinated notes (often called hybrids) come last among bondholders. Rating agencies notch subordinated debt below the issuer's senior rating for this reason.

Buyers pay up for a change of control put

Mixed   Expected: tighter

A change of control put lets holders sell the bonds back at 101% of face if the company is acquired and its rating falls below investment grade. It protects against a leveraged buyout loading the company with new debt. If buyers value it, bonds that carry one should price tighter.

Coupon steps mark weaker credits

Too early   Expected: wider

A coupon step raises the interest rate if the bonds are downgraded. It compensates holders for ratings risk, which is why it tends to appear when that risk is real: the step is a signal about the credit as much as a protection.

Acquisition debt carries a concession

Mixed   Expected: wider

The use of proceeds section of a prospectus says what the money is for. Debt that funds an acquisition adds leverage and integration risk; refinancing swaps old debt for new. If buyers read the section, acquisition deals should pay a concession.
